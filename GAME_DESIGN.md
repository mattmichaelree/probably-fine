# Game design

## Pitch

PROBABLY FINE is a comic 2D scenario-puzzle game in which a fairly normal Saturday becomes a chain of negotiations with labels, strangers, hunger, money, pets, bees, and someone saying "it should be fine." The joke is social confidence colliding with evidence.

The MVP is **Probably Fine: Saturday**: Grocery Store, Food Truck, Uncle Rick's BBQ, The Date, and The Biscuit. Aim for a 15-20 minute first run with immediate replay value. Each scene is a small, interactive place with props and people, not a text-only choice screen.

## Player promise

- Read a situation, inspect the environment, gather partial clues, and choose what to eat or do.
- Balance safety, hunger, satisfaction/boredom, cash, time, and relationships.
- Discover product and venue facts across runs, then use that knowledge to make smarter or funnier decisions.
- Sometimes take a knowingly stupid gamble with "Risk It for the Biscuit." The player must know it is a gamble.

## Core loop

1. Enter a scene with a clear, small goal.
2. Click/tap props and NPCs to reveal clues, alternatives, and pressure.
3. Spend time or money to inspect, ask, test where plausible, buy, substitute, or leave.
4. Commit to an action. Resolve condition rules and resource changes, show a specific reaction, then carry consequences forward.
5. Finish the scene, advance Saturday, and reveal a short consequence or discovery note.

## Resources and pressure

- **Money:** finite Saturday budget. Safe options may cost more; skipping food saves money but raises hunger.
- **Hunger:** increases with time and missed meals; high hunger makes tempting options more persuasive, not mechanically unavoidable.
- **Satisfaction / boredom:** the emotional pressure to choose something interesting. A safe but dull day can still be a valid win.
- **Time:** inspections, queues, travel, and awkward conversations consume it. Late arrival can change scene opportunities.
- **Inventory:** purchased snacks, substitutes, information artifacts, and condition-specific meds. Limited slots encourage choices, not busywork.
- **Condition load:** consequences of actual exposures, fatigue, and discomfort carried across scenes. Avoid arbitrary fatal outcomes.
- **Knowledge:** facts learned about ingredients, shared equipment, hosts, and environmental triggers persist across a run; selected discoveries may persist across replay if the design earns it.

## Conditions as rules

A character setup includes a compact set of conditions for the run. The first authored character should have at least one true food allergy, one intolerance, and one environmental trigger so different countermeasures matter. Exact default profile and severity tuning remain playtest decisions.

True food allergies can be triggered by ingredient or meaningful cross-contact. Intolerances have dose/threshold consequences and may have relevant aids. Environmental triggers include cat dander, pollen/cedar, and bees as scenario hazards. The player should be able to see why an outcome happened in plain language. No medicine converts an unsafe allergy exposure into a safe meal.

## Information states

For each edible object, separate **actual facts** from **what the player knows**. A label may disclose ingredients but not a shared fryer. An NPC can be confident and wrong. A test/inspection can narrow uncertainty, not always erase it. UI should mark known, suspected, and unknown facts without revealing hidden truth prematurely.

## Humor and outcomes

Uncle Rick is a well-meaning confidence machine whose recipe history is less reliable than his delivery. The game laughs at certainty, social pressure, and the player's own gambles, not at illness. Favor short visual punchlines: a dramatic close-up on a tiny ingredient, a fryer whispering "same oil," Rick producing a second, even less helpful label. Endings should be specific to choices and legible in under a minute.

## Exclusions for MVP

No job system, town map, open-ended days, crafting tree, online account, multiplayer, realistic medical simulator, or giant choice catalog. Work and broader social life can become later content only if the five-scene Saturday is already fun.
